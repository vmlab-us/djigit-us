Leasing a Ford F-150

An F-150 lease must account for annual mileage, expected wear, work use, modifications, bed condition, trailer use, and the exact rules in the contract. Buying a new versus used F-150

For a new F-150, verify cab, bed, engine, drivetrain, axle, payload label, towing equipment, trim, and factory options for the exact VIN. For a used truck, inspect service and use history, frame and underbody, hitch and bed wear, tires, modifications, collision repairs, and whether the claimed capability matches its labels.

Inspect the frame, bed, hitch, suspension, tires, fluid leaks, maintenance records, modifications, off-road evidence, electronic towing aids, and payload labels. A vehicle-history report is useful but cannot replace a physical inspection, document review, and test drive appropriate to the full-size pickup. Confirm that recalls and service campaigns have been addressed where applicable.

F-150 ownership costs and practical checks
