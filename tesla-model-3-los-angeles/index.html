Compare selling price, down payment, annual percentage rate, term, taxes, registration, documented fees, optional products, trade equity, amount financed, monthly payment, and total of payments.

Buying a new versus used Model 3

For a new Model 3, verify drive layout, wheels, interior, estimated range, charging hardware, order fees, and included software. For a used Model 3, confirm build date, warranty status, battery behavior, charging speed, tires, underbody and wheel condition, repair history, and feature transferability.

Review warranty coverage, high-voltage alerts, charging history where available, glass and wheel damage, tires, suspension noise, collision repairs, and app-transfer readiness. A vehicle-history report is useful but cannot replace a physical inspection, document review, and test drive appropriate to the electric sedan. Confirm that recalls and service campaigns have been addressed where applicable.

Model 3 ownership costs and practical checks

Total cost deserves the same attention as trim and color. Include electricity, home-equipment installation, insurance, registration, tires, paid connectivity, public-charging fees, and possible parking costs in the Model 3 budget. Request insurance estimates for the actual configuration because trim, drivetrain, safety equipment, repair costs, location, driving record, and coverage can materially change the premium.

Trade-in planning for a Model 3

Positive equity may reduce a Model 3 balance; negative equity may increase it and affect approval. DJIGIT US shows the connection without guaranteeing an early appraisal. Review final figures in writing.
